PROJECT 02

mythOS

The idea of an operating system

An operating system in name, an experiment in mind. mythOS is where I question what an OS is actually for — looked at from the ground up, and out loud.

VII

What if an operating system could be small enough to understand completely?

All builds
I

The question.

What is an operating system actually for? Not which features it ships, but what it owes the person using it — and what it quietly costs the machine. mythOS is how I hold that question still long enough to look at it.

PLACEHOLDER — the framing, the starting problem.
II

Why not the defaults.

Today's systems are built by enormous teams for enormous numbers of people. I want to know what a system looks like when it is designed to be understood by one person — and what that one person loses and gains in exchange.

PLACEHOLDER — what "not the defaults" means here, concretely.
III

A small universe.

An operating system is a universe in miniature — memory, processes, files, permissions, a clock. Every rule of that universe is documented somewhere, and I want to read those rules slowly, in order, and then rebuild them.

PLACEHOLDER — the map of the universe this project is exploring.
IV

The build, honestly.

Sketching, poking at what already exists, taking it apart and reassembling it my way. Honest state: early days — and early days are the best days to ask stupid questions loudly.

PLACEHOLDER — tools, repositories, screenshots, progress.
V

What I want to learn.

What actually has to happen to boot something you understand? What breaks when an entire system is one person's mental model? Whether this ends in a working system or a working understanding — the trip is the point.

PLACEHOLDER — the lessons, added as they arrive.
