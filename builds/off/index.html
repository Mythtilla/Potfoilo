PROJECT 01

OFF

Private communication

A private way to talk. Small and quiet and built to understand what communication is like when nothing is watching.

Year
2026
Status
In progress
Role
Solo — all of it, for now
Tech
TypeScript · Vite · Supabase
All builds
01.

A private way to talk.

The working premise is in the name: communication that is off — off the default networks, off other people's ledgers, off somebody else's clock. What that means in detail is being written as I go.

The concrete shape: a small community platform where you enter with a pseudonym and a password — nothing else required. A seeded public World room, interest rooms, private rooms, and a request-based DM flow. It should feel like a room you borrowed, not a product that borrowed you.
02.

Why it exists.

Most conversations now run through rooms that have landlords. I want a room without one — something that belongs to the people talking, not to the company hosting the talk. OFF is the attempt to draw that room.

It started where the frustration was: every serious conversation I have now happens in a room with a landlord. OFF exists to practice the alternative — somewhere the people talking hold the walls, not a company holding the ledger.
03.

The problem I keep circling.

Most messaging tools solve the same problem: more people talking more often. OFF is only interested in one question — can people talk freely? I think those are different problems, and I think the second one is abandoned.

Most messaging products optimise for more people talking more often. OFF asks only whether people can talk freely — with trust enforced by the system, not promised by the company. That is the part most existing answers quietly skip.
04.

What has been built so far.

A working first version now exists: rooms, password-only auth, realtime chat, and recovery. This section will keep moving as the build moves — in public, as notes, with all the mess kept in.

What is real: a Vite + TypeScript client, Supabase for database, auth and realtime, password-only authentication, a two-step onboarding with a recovery phrase, an honest connection indicator, and optimistic messaging inside rooms gatekept by Postgres RLS. The code lives in the open at github.com/Mythtilla/OFF.
05.

Decisions still on the table.

What “off” means exactly. Where the messages live. Who can read them. Whether it should be a protocol or a place. Whether it needs to be finished at all — or just understood.

Still open: a request-inbox UI, a blocking UI, moderation, invitations, receipts, and account deletion. DMs are currently a request flow, not open to anyone. End-to-end encryption has a design document and a roadmap — it is not shipped yet, and says so.
06.

Security — and privacy as a default.

The bar I keep reaching for: if a stranger reads it, that is a failure of design, not a surprise. Privacy is not a feature to bolt on later here. It is the reason the project exists.

The threat model is written into the repo: the client carries only a publishable key, with no service-role path. Postgres RLS is the authority and is never disabled; sensitive writes go through security-definer RPCs that reject unauthenticated callers. No analytics, no fingerprinting, no IP reads. Account recovery stores only a one-way bcrypt verifier of the recovery phrase, and resets revoke every session.
07.

Where it stands now.

In progress, and honest. The next entries in this section will be about moderation, an inbox, and accounts that can leave. Until then, what is missing says so visibly.

In progress, and running code, not paper: pseudonymous sign-in, the seeded World room, optimistic chat, and recovery all work. Next on the shelf: moderation, an inbox, account deletion, and the E2EE roadmap. Progress lands publicly in github.com/Mythtilla/OFF.

The quietest channel is the one that belongs to nobody's ledger.
