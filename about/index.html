About The person behind the keys

A student who builds.

I am a student. That is the whole job description, and I intend to keep it. I spend most of my time with computers — not because they are the point, but because they are the fastest way I know to turn a question into something I can hold, inspect, and take apart.

I learn by making. Reading gives me the shape of an idea, but the idea only becomes mine once I have rebuilt it, broken it, and put it back together slightly differently. This site is that habit applied to myself: the thinking and the experiments and the projects are all notes from a workshop that happens to be online.

What pulls my attention: things that run quietly, interfaces that don't beg, systems small enough to understand entirely, the way words sit on a page, and the open question of what technology would look like if it weren't a business first.

I am also a student of a language with no alphabet. Japanese has been the slowest, most humbling systems project I have started — you learn a piece, and the pieces do not compose the way you hoped. I am years from good, and I like knowing that.

Where it all goes: nowhere in particular, on purpose. I want to understand computers well enough to build something that feels personal — and to remain the kind of person who asks before assuming. That is the whole ambition, and I hope it stays small.
