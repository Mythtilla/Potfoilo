A digital notebook Kept slowly

THINK/ING

These are the fragments I keep coming back to. They are not wisdom. They are working notes — often wrong, occasionally revised, always mine.

Most of what I believe is probably wrong.
That is not a problem.
It is the starting point.

01

Question the defaults.

Most systems run on choices no one made — data retention, password rules, column width, why a button is where it is. Asking “why is it this way” is cheap and it keeps returning answers like “we've always done it”. I try to keep that reflex switched on.
02

Build to understand, not just to ship.

The reason I make things is the understanding that comes out of them. That changes the finish line: a project is done when it is understood, not when it is released. Some of my best projects ended early, understood.
03

Understanding is reproduction.

Reading feels like knowing. It isn't. The honest test is closed-book: explain it, rebuild it, break it and watch how it breaks. Everything I am actually confident about survives that test. Most of the rest was recognition wearing a disguise.
04

Small, private, quiet.

Almost everything I make for my own reasons is small and private and quiet. I don't mean this as a distance from the noisy internet. I mean it as a preference for a room that has no audience.
05

Change your mind, out loud.

Evidence that contradicts me is not a threat to me. It is an upgrade that arrived in the mail. I try to revise my positions loudly enough that other people notice that revising is allowed.
06

Silence is a material.

An empty region of a page is not the absence of a design. It is part of the design. The best layouts let the space talk — and the quiet sections of a site are often the ones people remember.
07

Stay a student.

A student who finishes knowing is done. A student who keeps asking is just getting started. I intend to carry the asking with me, on purpose, for as long as I can.
08

I make things because that's how I think.

Making is a way of finishing a sentence that my brain couldn't finish on its own. The hands are part of the thinking. This site is one long proof of that.

Questions I am sitting with

Q1

Why do operating systems grow until they have to be replaced?

Q2

Can privacy be a default instead of a feature you switch on?

Q3

What is worth automating — and what is worth keeping in my hands?

Q4

Is the internet permanently loud, or is that just this one loud era?

Q5

What would software look like if nobody was watching?

Q6

What does it mean to build something that makes you unnecessary?

Working principles

P1Understand before optimizingSpeed is nothing if it's speeding the wrong thing.
P2Prefer the simple explanationComplexity is a smell until proven otherwise.
P3Keep notes, even bad onesHalf a thought saved beats a full one forgotten.
P4Make it quiet before making it cleverRestraint first. The rest is decoration.
P5Leave the place better than you found itSmall, kind, unannounced.
P6When in doubt, build a small oneA small thing that works beats a big thing that waits.

That's the notebook so far.
It gets longer.
